The Linear Interpolation Concept

To find the square root of a non-perfect square, anchor it between the two nearest perfect squares you already know by heart, then apply a linear adjustment.

3-Step Tutorial

  1. Find the closest lower perfect square (a) and upper perfect square (b).
  2. Take the root of the lower square (\sqrt{a}) as your base whole number.
  3. Add the fractional adjustment using the formula: \sqrt{x} \approx \sqrt{a} + \frac{x - a}{2\sqrt{a}}

Tutorial 1: Anchoring Square Bounds

Identify surrounding perfect squares to frame your decimal estimate tightly.

Example: Estimate \sqrt{68}

1. Nearest squares are 64 (\sqrt{64} = 8) and 81 (\sqrt{81} = 9).

2. Base root is 8. Difference from 64 is 68 - 64 = 4.

3. Calculation: 8 + \frac{4}{16} = 8 + 0.25 = 8.25.

Tutorial 2: Small Offset Adjustments

For numbers very close to a lower square, the linear offset formula gives an accurate decimal instantly.

Example: Estimate \sqrt{50}

1. Lower square is 49 (\sqrt{49} = 7). Difference is 1.

2. Double the base root: 2 \times 7 = 14.

3. Calculation: 7 + \frac{1}{14} \approx 7.07.
