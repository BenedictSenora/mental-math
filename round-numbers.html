Working with Large Round Numbers

The Round-and-Adjust Framework

Rounding awkward multipliers up to clean decades or hundreds makes mental arithmetic nearly instantaneous, provided you compensate at the end.

Formula: A \times (100 - X) = (A \times 100) - (A \times X).

3-Step Tutorial

  1. Round an awkward multiplier up to the nearest clean decade or hundred (e.g., treat 98 as 100).
  2. Multiply by the round number for an easy initial product.
  3. Subtract the extra amount you added to find the final adjusted answer.

Tutorial 1: Multiplying by Numbers Near 100

Round numbers like 98 or 99 up to 100 to simplify complex products.

Example: 34 × 98

1. Treat 98 as 100.

2. Multiply: 34 \times 100 = 3,400.

3. Subtract two 34s: 3,400 - 68 = 3,332.

Tutorial 2: Multiplying by Numbers Near 50

Adapt the rounding technique for multipliers close to 50 by using half of 100.

Example: 26 × 49

1. Treat 49 as 50.

2. Multiply: (26 \times 100) \div 2 = 1,300.

3. Subtract one 26: 1,300 - 26 = 1,274.
