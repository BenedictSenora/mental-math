Divisibility Rules for 3, 6, and 9

Divisibility & Fractions Module 1 of 4

The Digit Sum Concept

Instead of executing long division, you can instantly determine if a massive number divides evenly by 3, 6, or 9 simply by adding up its individual digits.

3-Step Tutorial

  1. Add all individual digits of the target number together.
  2. Check if the sum is divisible by 3 (for rule of 3) or 9 (for rule of 9).
  3. For the rule of 6, ensure the number is also even (ends in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8).

Tutorial 1: Rules for 3 and 9

Sum the digits recursively until you get a single-digit number to verify divisibility.

Example: Is 4,287 divisible by 9?

1. Add digits: 4 + 2 + 8 + 7 = 21.

2. Add digits again: 2 + 1 = 3.

3. Since 3 is not divisible by 9, 4,287 is not divisible by 9.

Tutorial 2: Rule for 6 (Even + Divisible by 3)

A number is divisible by 6 if and only if it passes both the even-number test and the rule of 3.

Example: Is 2,316 divisible by 6?

1. Check even: Ends in 6 (Yes, it is even).

2. Check rule of 3: 2 + 3 + 1 + 6 = 12 (Divisible by 3).

3. 2,316 is fully divisible by 6.

Quick Practice Checks

Practice 1: Is 5,436 divisible by 9?

Click to Reveal Solution

5 + 4 + 3 + 6 = 18 (1 + 8 = 9). Yes, it is fully divisible by 9!

Practice 2: Is 412 divisible by 6?

Click to Reveal Solution

It is even, but 4 + 1 + 2 = 7 (not divisible by 3). Therefore, No.
