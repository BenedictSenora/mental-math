Sales Tax & Discount Calculation

Real-World Applications Module 3 of 4

The Complementary Percentage Concept

When calculating sale prices (like "30% off"), don't calculate the discount and subtract it—instead, directly calculate the remaining percentage you actually pay (70%).

Formula: \text{Sale Price} = \text{Original Price} \times (100\% - \text{Discount}\%).

3-Step Tutorial

  1. Subtract the discount percentage from 100% to find the portion you pay.
  2. Convert that percentage into a decimal multiplier (e.g., 70% becomes 0.7).
  3. Multiply the original price by that factor.

Tutorial 1: Direct Discount Pricing

Skip the subtraction step at the end by multiplying by the complementary percentage right away.

Example: A $50 Shirt at 30% Off

1. Since it is 30% off, you pay 70% ($100\% - 30\%$).

2. Multiply: $50 × 0.70 = $35.00 final price.

Tutorial 2: Quick Sales Tax Estimation

For a standard ~8% or 10% sales tax, find 10% first and adjust slightly downward if needed.

Example: $120 Item with 10% Tax

1. Find 10% tax: $12.00.

2. Add to original price: $120 + $12 = $132.00 total.

Quick Practice Checks

Practice 1: How much do you pay for a $40 jacket at 20% off?

Click to Reveal Solution

You pay 80% → $40 × 0.80 = $32.00.

Practice 2: What is the total cost of a $90 pair of shoes with 10% tax?

Click to Reveal Solution

10% tax is $9 → $90 + $9 = $99.00.
