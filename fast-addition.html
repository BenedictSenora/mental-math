Fast Addition: Left-to-Right Method

Core Arithmetic Module 1 of 6

Why Traditional Addition Is Slow

In school, you learned to add numbers from right to left (ones column first, then carrying over). That works on paper, but in your head, it forces you to hold numbers backward.

Speed math flips the process: always add from left to right by breaking numbers into place values (hundreds, tens, ones).

3-Step Tutorial

  1. Split the second number into tens and ones (or hundreds, tens, and ones).
  2. Add the largest place value first to the first number to get a running total.
  3. Add the remaining smaller digits to your running total to get the final answer.

Tutorial 1: Left-to-Right Addition

Always add numbers from left to right (tens first, then ones) so you calculate the most significant digits immediately.

Example: 47 + 38

1. Split 38 into 30 + 8.

2. Add tens: 47 + 30 = 77.

3. Add ones: 77 + 8 = 85.

Tutorial 2: The Compensation Method

Round one number up to the nearest clean decade (like 10, 20, or 50), do the easy addition, and subtract the extra amount at the end.

Example: 56 + 39

1. Treat 39 as 40 (added 1 extra).

2. Add: 56 + 40 = 96.

3. Subtract the extra 1: 95.

Quick Practice Checks
