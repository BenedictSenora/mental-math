The Ceiling-Rounding Concept

Avoid checkout surprises by rounding item prices up to the nearest whole dollar as you place items in your cart.

Pattern: Always rounding up creates an automatic safety buffer that covers local sales tax.

3-Step Tutorial

  1. Round every item's price up to the next whole dollar (e.g., $3.29 becomes $4).
  2. Keep a running left-to-right total in your head as you shop.
  3. Treat the excess cents from rounding up as your built-in tax cushion.

Tutorial 1: Round-Up Running Total

Never try to add cents in your head while walking through store aisles—convert everything to whole integers.

Example: Cart Items ($4.89, $2.15, $6.49)

1. Round each price up: $5 + $3 + $7.

2. Add whole dollars: $5 + $3 = $8, then + $7 = $15.00 safe estimate.

Tutorial 2: Clustering Dollar Pairs

Group items whose cents complement each other (e.g., .75 and .25) into clean whole dollars before adding.

Example: $3.75 and $4.25

1. Combine the cents: $.75 + $.25 = $1.00.

2. Add whole dollars: $3 + $4 + $1 = $8.00 exact.
