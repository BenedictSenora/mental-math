Divisibility Rules for 4 and 8

Divisibility & Fractions Module 2 of 4

The Ending Digits Concept

You don't need to look at the entire large number; you only need to examine the final few tail digits to test for powers of 2 like 4 and 8.

3-Step Tutorial

  1. Isolate the tail digits of the given number.
  2. Check the last 2 digits against 4, or the last 3 digits against 8.
  3. Perform quick mental division on those tail digits alone.

Tutorial 1: The Rule for 4

Look strictly at the last 2 digits. If that two-digit number divides evenly by 4, the whole number does.

Example: Is 73,524 divisible by 4?

1. Isolate the last two digits: 24.

2. Divide by 4: 24 \div 4 = 6 evenly.

3. Conclusion: 73,524 is divisible by 4.

Tutorial 2: The Rule for 8

Look strictly at the last 3 digits. If that three-digit value divides evenly by 8, the entire number does.

Example: Is 51,216 divisible by 8?

1. Isolate the last three digits: 216.

2. Divide by 8: 216 \div 8 = 27 evenly.

3. Conclusion: 51,216 is divisible by 8.

Quick Practice Checks

Practice 1: Is 9,312 divisible by 4?
